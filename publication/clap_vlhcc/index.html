A Large-Scale Empirical Study on Android Runtime Permission Rationale Messages

Xueqing Liu, Yue Leng, Wei Yang, Wenyu Wang, Chengxiang Zhai, Tao Xie

In the IEEE Symposium on Visual Languages and Human-Centric Computing.

Pdf

Abstract

After Android 6.0 introduces the runtimepermission system, many apps provide runtime-permissiongroup rationales for the users to better understand the permissions requested by the apps. To understand the patterns of rationales and to what extent the rationales can improve the users’ understanding of the purposes of requesting permission groups, we conduct a large-scale measurement study on five aspects of runtime rationales. We have five main findings: (1) less than 25% apps under study provide rationales; (2) for permission-group purposes that are difficult to understand, the proportions of apps that provide rationales are even lower; (3) the purposes stated in a significant proportion of rationales are incorrect; (4) a large proportion of customized rationales do not provide more information than the default permission-requesting message of Android; (5) apps that provide rationales are more likely to explain the same permission group’s purposes in their descriptions than apps that do not provide rationales. We further discuss important implications from these findings.
